About Glastonbury

Three hundred and ninety years of river town, in three short sections.

Town Overview

History

Glastonbury was first settled in 1636, when thirty families from Wethersfield took up a tract of land called Pyaug on the eastern bank of the Connecticut River. The town was incorporated in 1693, and renamed to its present spelling in 1870 after Glastonbury in Somerset, England.

Because it grew along a main road rather than around a village green, Glastonbury looks a little different from the postcard image of a New England town — a small green was only established in the middle of town in the mid-twentieth century. What it has instead is the river. The Rocky Hill–Glastonbury Ferry, the oldest continuously operating ferry in the United States, still crosses from South Glastonbury as it has since 1655.

The town's later history is industrial: eighteenth- and early nineteenth-century shipbuilding on the river, then the J.B. Williams Soap Company, which began making shaving soap here in the 1840s and grew into an international business. Parts of the old soap factory complex now stand as the Soap Factory Condominiums, and J.B. Williams Park on Neipsic Road carries the founder's name.

Population

35,159

The population of Glastonbury at the 2020 U.S. Census, in a town of 52.2 square miles. That is roughly 686 people per square mile, and it places Glastonbury among the larger towns in the Capitol Planning Region.

Source: 2020 U.S. Census, via Wikipedia ↗opens in a new tab

Government

Glastonbury is governed under the Council–Manager form of local government. A nine-member Town Council sets policy and appoints a Town Manager, who runs day-to-day town operations. The current Town Manager is Jonathan Luiz.

Town Hall is at 2155 Main Street, Glastonbury, CT 06033, and is open Monday through Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., excluding national holidays. The main number is (860) 652-7710.
